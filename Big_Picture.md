# Big Picture: Spatial Diffusion in Electric School Bus Adoption

## Core Question

> Do public subsidies for electric school buses generate **local diffusion spillovers** beyond directly funded districts?

---

## Paper Narrative

### 1. Motivation: spatial clustering is visible

Start with the empirical fact:

> ESB adoption is geographically clustered.

Then ask:

> Is this clustering causal diffusion, or just correlated demand / institutional sorting?

---

### 2. Conceptual framework: channels of local diffusion

Use "spatial spillovers" as the umbrella term. Then distinguish channels:

**A. Information / peer-learning spillovers**
Districts learn from nearby adopters about:
- technology quality
- charging logistics
- procurement process
- operating costs
- grant-writing/admin burden

**B. Demonstration / salience effects**
Nearby buses make the technology visible and politically salient. Districts may not learn technical facts, but adoption becomes more legitimate.

**C. Supply-side / vendor activation spillovers**
Vendors enter or intensify effort in an area after a nearby funded sale:
- outreach
- service network
- dealer relationships
- procurement support
- charging coordination

**D. Infrastructure / ecosystem spillovers**
Nearby adoption may lower fixed costs of:
- charging infrastructure knowledge
- maintenance capacity
- utility coordination
- local technician availability

---

### 3. Empirical object: total local diffusion effect

> The primary estimand is not pure peer learning. It is the total causal effect of nearby subsidized adoption activity on own adoption.

---

## Paper Structure

### 1. Introduction
- ESB adoption is spatially clustered
- Subsidies may create spillovers beyond treated districts
- But clustering may reflect sorting, not causal diffusion
- Use CSBP lottery variation to test causal local diffusion
- Main result: little evidence of diffusion after correcting for nonrandom exposure

### 2. Institutional Background
- ESB market
- CSBP subsidies
- R1 lottery
- Why local spillovers could matter

### 3. Conceptual Channels
1. Information / peer learning
2. Demonstration / salience
3. Vendor activation
4. Infrastructure / ecosystem spillovers

### 4. Descriptive Evidence: Spatial Clustering in ESB Adoption
- Moran's I
- Neighbor adoption gradients
- Maps
- Raw KNN correlations

### 5. Research Design: Lottery-Based Evidence on Causal Diffusion
- Nearby R1 winners as exogenous shocks
- Concern: nonrandom exposure
- BH recentering / counterfactual lottery exposure

### 6. Results

**A. Raw spatial correlations** — Strong positive gradients.

**B. Lottery-based estimates** — Reduced-form positive, but nonrandom exposure suspected.

**C. Recentered lottery estimates** — Null.

Main interpretation:
> Spatial clustering appears driven more by applicant geography and correlated adoption propensity than by causal local diffusion.

### 7. Mechanisms and Interpretation
The design estimates total local diffusion. The null suggests neither peer-learning nor supply-side activation generates detectable adoption responses at this scale.

### 8. Conclusion
- Spatial clustering does not imply causal diffusion
- Subsidies may not generate large local multiplier effects
- Policy implication: relying on organic diffusion may be insufficient

**Use:** spatial diffusion, local spillovers, technology adoption spillovers, subsidy-induced diffusion,
local treatment spillovers, information spillovers, demonstration effects, vendor activation,
market development spillovers, ecosystem spillovers

**Avoid as umbrella terms:** peer effects, social learning, contagion (too narrow unless mechanism is isolated)
