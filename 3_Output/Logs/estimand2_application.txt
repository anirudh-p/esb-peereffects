======================================================================
R2 GRANT NEIGHBOUR ANALYSIS
======================================================================
  R2 grantees are ~2023 adopters whose grants were awarded BEFORE R3 opened.
  Districts that observe a R2-grantee neighbour may be more encouraged to
  apply to R3 independently of R1 lottery variation.  Two robustness checks:

── (a) Heterogeneity split: R2-neighbour present vs absent (K=6, loser=Yes) ──
  R2-neighbour present: N=1,140  R3-apply rate=4.47%
  R2-neighbour absent : N=11,248  R3-apply rate=5.03%
    R2-nbr present: coef=+0.06494  SE=0.06578  p=0.3236  N=1,140
    R2-nbr absent : coef=+0.04575  SE=0.03390  p=0.1771  N=11,248

── (b) Additive R2-neighbour control — does R1 coefficient change? (K=6, loser=Yes) ──
    K=6 loser=Yes without R2 ctrl: coef=+0.05075  SE=0.02907  p=0.0809*
    K=6 loser=Yes with R2 ctrl   : coef=+0.05252  SE=0.02898  p=0.0699*
  If R1 coef stable after adding R2 control → R1 peer effect is not proxying
  for local propensity to adopt driven by nearby R2 grants.

======================================================================
APPLICANT-EXPOSED SAMPLE  (K=6, loser=Yes)
======================================================================
  Restrict to districts that had at least one R1-applicant neighbour,
  i.e. w6_IV_Z_R1 > 0  OR  w6_IS_R1_LOSER > 0.
  This sharpens the comparison to winner-neighbour vs loser-neighbour
  districts, removing the 'never-exposed' remote districts from the
  control group.  The coefficient on w_IS_R1_LOSER tests discouragement:
  a negative sign means observing a lottery loser next door reduces own
  R3 application propensity conditioning on winner share.

  Applicant-exposed N=7,085  (vs full sample N=12,388)
  R3-apply rate in exposed sample: 5.31%

  w_IV_Z_R1  (winner share):  coef=+0.04039  SE=0.03850  p=0.2942  N=7,085
  w_r1_loser (loser share):   coef=+0.00583  SE=0.02106  p=0.7820
  Negative w_r1_loser coef would support a discouragement mechanism.

======================================================================
DISTANCE-DECAY ROBUSTNESS  (K=6, loser=Yes)
======================================================================
  Replace uniform 1/K weights with inverse-distance normalised weights
  (wd6_IV_Z_R1 = Σ (1/d_ij / Σ 1/d_ij) × Z_j for K=6 neighbours).
  If the peer effect is driven by proximity rather than a flat network,
  the distance-decay coefficient should be larger in magnitude.
    Uniform 1/K (w6) : coef=+0.05075  SE=0.02907  p=0.0809*  N=12,388
    Inv-dist  (wd6): coef=+0.03690  SE=0.02839  p=0.1936  N=12,388
